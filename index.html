About

I specialize in Information Architecture to support access and governance across public-facing educational and civic technologies. Bringing an interdisciplinary Data Science background, my goal is to co-develop data governance strategies alongside affected communities and implement them on open-source platforms.

My work bridges computational trace data analysis, critical interaction design, and participatory auditing—investigating how we can preserve "productive friction" within automated systems to foster metacognition, agency, and digital equity.

Research & Publications

EthicsBot: Open-Source LLMs for Research Ethics CSCW 2026 Workshop

First-authored study benchmarking open-source LLMs to provide real-time, context-aware ethical guidance for CS researchers. Evaluated tool adoption across 16 interdisciplinary expert interviews to map variations in user learning needs.

Participatory Action Research in AI Literacy MIT Media Lab (Personal Robots Group)

Facilitated participatory action research (PAR) seminars for K-12 educators across 11 countries, using empathy mapping, card sorting, and peer algorithmic auditing to surface localized community concerns on classroom AI.

Taxonomies of AI Fear & Pedagogical Interventions Independent Research Study

Mining online student discourse via web scraping and thematic coding to categorize public mental models of AI anxiety, designing educational interventions that empower learners to build critical literacy.

Praxis & Public Policy

Control Arms (Geneva) — Coordination & Design Albright Institute Fellow

Restructured frontend web assets to optimize low-bandwidth accessibility for international delegates in developing nations. Facilitated consultations on gender-based violence across the Asia-Pacific, Africa, and Latin America to align regional practice with UN policy.

Interactive Web Cookies Privacy Tool Streamlit / Python

Co-designed an open-source interactive web application deployed on Streamlit, allowing users to upload browser data and engage with dynamic visual representations of digital tracking and data privacy.

CS Department Inclusivity Curriculum Module Wellesley College

Initiated and authored a curriculum module examining power, privilege, and oppressive systems within computer science, integrated into intermediate computer science coursework.
